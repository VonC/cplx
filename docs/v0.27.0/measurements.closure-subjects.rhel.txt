Probe: what a DT_NEEDED walk from the entry points reaches, and what it misses.
       Asked for by round 1 of the design review of
       design.v0.27.0.toolchain-runtime-closure.md
Target: RHEL 9.8, the live deployment install of the published archive
Captured: 2026-09-03, by hand over ssh

WHY IT WAS TAKEN. The design proposed a subject set built from the transitive
       DT_NEEDED closure of the interpreter and the shipped executables. The
       review refused it: CPython loads extension modules with dlopen, and a
       DT_NEEDED walk cannot reach an object nothing declares. If that is true,
       the guarantee would be blind to the wheel and extension objects that
       motivated the whole cross-distribution effort.

       It is true, and the size of the gap is the point.

THE WALK IS DELIBERATELY GENEROUS. It matches each DT_NEEDED name against any
       file of that basename anywhere under the tools tree, not only within the
       resolution scope. A stricter, scope-only walk can only reach FEWER
       objects, so every miss recorded here is a miss under any stricter rule
       as well.

WHAT IT MEASURED:

       628 shipped ELF objects under the tools tree.
       185 entry points, executables under a bin or sbin directory.
       176 distinct names reached by the transitive walk.
       395 shipped ELFs NOT reached, of which
        76 are CPython extension modules under lib-dynload and
        28 are under site-packages.
         0 shipped objects declare a cpython extension module as DT_NEEDED.

       A subject set built from that walk alone would never examine any of the
       395, including every extension module the interpreter dlopens. The
       requirement's own evidence, the pymupdf and manylinux wheel failures,
       lives entirely inside that blind spot.
