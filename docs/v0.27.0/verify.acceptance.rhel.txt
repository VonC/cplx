This is the RHEL half of the step 5 evidence and the only half that can carry the
equivalence comparison, which needs two engines over one archive on one host.

**The equivalence verdict states what it measured.** Seven fields are compared
exactly with no allowance of any kind. mtime is compared exactly on 30073 of
30665 entries. The remaining 592 are the entries the post-copy passes rewrite,
and each must fall inside the wall-clock window of its own install. That set is
not declared: it is measured, by installing a third time with the same engine and
taking the entries that move between two runs of it.

**The loader line is `5 and 5`, and the previous run's `5 and 4` is why this
capture exists.** The loader was located by an unsorted lookup, and the published
tree carries one per toolchain root, so directory order decided which loader each
probe used. Two probes of two provably identical trees then read the same ELF
with different loaders. The old verdict compared the selected ELF but never the
loader, and read only the unresolved totals, which were zero on both sides, so it
passed on measurements that were never comparable. Both lookups are now sorted,
the loader is required to match, and a differing dependency count under the same
ELF and loader is a failure.

**Seven of the fourteen negative controls now drive oracles with fabricated
inputs**, because the accepting branch is the only one a real archive can produce.
Two feed the equivalence oracle a defect placed on a control-noisy path; five
feed the loader verdict an absent fallback, a mismatched ELF, a mismatched
loader, a differing dependency count and a listing too short to compare. All
refuse.

**Recovery is decided against the archive, not against the tree it replaced.**
`accept rerun restores the tree` compares the recovered tree with the archive's
own entry listing, produced by tar rather than by the installer. The earlier form
passed whenever the rerun added any entry at all, so a rerun reaching three
entries of nine would have passed a case labelled complete recovery.

every review round in steps 4 and 5 moved the harness or the documentation, never
the installer. The harness whole-file digest `d216e2a3` pins this capture to the
staged cplx source; the shared-body digest `ccf52d7a` is the value the Debian
capture also reports, which is what proves the two targets ran the same harness.
