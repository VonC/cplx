ROUND 6 FOUND THE ROUND 5 OWNERSHIP RULE READING THE WRONG THING, and this
         capture is the round 6 run. The rule attributed a process by the PATH
         in its command line, and the reviewer's reproduction showed what that
         misses: a venv entry point is a SHELL WRAPPER that starts the real
         interpreter by its ABSOLUTE CANDIDATE PATH, so the child's arguments
         name the candidate and never the venv. A wrapper's path is not
         inherited by its child.

         process exited before anything of it could be observed" while a
         CANDIDATE-MAPPED child of its own launch was running and unowned: a
         missed success, not only an unreaped survivor. The committed control
         hid it, because its plant supplied the marker the rule needed.

         OWNERSHIP IS NOW TAKEN FROM THE LAUNCH AND FROM NOTHING ELSE, by two
         independent properties of it.

         THE PROCESS GROUP. The launch is made with job control on, so the
         launched process becomes a group leader and its group id IS its pid.
         Every descendant inherits it, across `exec`, across the parent's exit
         and across reparenting to PID 1, because a process group outlives the
         process that created it. Nothing else on the machine can be in it.

         THE ENVIRONMENT NONCE. The launch carries a variable no other process
         has, `exec` preserves the environment, and a child that leaves the
         group by calling `setsid` still carries it.

         Neither depends on an argument, which is the assumption round 6
         destroyed. The helper's own shell and PID 1 are never owned.

         name it on the `OWNED` record, reach a typed verdict and reap it. The

         THE EARLY EXIT, with a survivor OUTSIDE the candidate started by

         candidate, so the helper must refuse and must still sweep it.
